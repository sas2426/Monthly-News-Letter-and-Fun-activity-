SpaceYMe Monthly Edition · November 2026

Fireballs, a Planet Meet & a Supermoon

Space · Aerospace · Science · Research — the Taurids kick off the month, Mars meets Jupiter before dawn, and the Beaver supermoon rises beside the Pleiades.

One of the two closest full Moons of 2026 — slightly larger and brighter than usual.

Segment 01 — SkyWatch Highlights

What to look up for this month

November rewards early risers: the best planets sit in the east before dawn. A new Moon on Nov 9 gives the darkest skies of the month.

NOV 4–5 & 11–12 · LATE EVENING

Taurid fireballs

Southern Taurids peak Nov 4–5 and Northern Taurids Nov 11–12. Only about 5 meteors an hour, but they're famous for bright, slow fireballs radiating from near the Pleiades — and both peaks fall around a dim Moon.

no equipment needed
NOV 16 · PRE-DAWN

Mars meets Jupiter

The two planets pass just about 1.2° apart in the eastern morning sky, near the bright star Regulus in Leo. They return with the Moon on Nov 30 — an easy pair from India too, before sunrise.

naked eye
NOV 17–18 · AFTER MIDNIGHT

Leonids — dark skies, modest show

Fast meteors from Leo, around 10–15 an hour at best. The Moon sets early, so the window from about 2 a.m. to dawn is dark. Not a storm year — but a pleasant, uncrowded watch.

no equipment needed
NOV 24 · EVENING

Beaver supermoon + Pleiades

The first supermoon since January rises close to the Pleiades cluster. Moonlight will drown the cluster's faint stars, but the pairing is a lovely naked-eye and photo moment.

naked eye
NOV 25–26 · ALL NIGHT

Uranus at opposition

Uranus sits opposite the Sun in Taurus, as close and bright as it gets this year (magnitude about 5.6). Binoculars or a telescope will show a steady blue-green dot — easier on moonless nights, so try around Nov 9 or in early December.

binoculars or telescope
Segment 02 — Aerospace & Missions

Settling in, checking out, getting ready

November is a quieter, in-between month in orbit — new crews and new telescopes settling into their work.

NASA · COMMISSIONING

🇺🇸Roman Space Telescope checks out its instruments

Launched Aug 30 on a Falcon Heavy, Roman heads for (and then operates near) the Sun–Earth L2 point, about a million miles away. A multi-month commissioning phase tests the Wide Field Instrument and Coronagraph before science surveys for dark energy and exoplanets begin.

ISS · EXPEDITION ONGOING

🇺🇸Crew-13 settles into a six-month stay

The crew that docked on Oct 1 — Jessica Watkins, Luke Delaney, Sergey Teteryatnikov and CSA's Joshua Kutryk — is now into its long-duration science and maintenance routine aboard the station.

ISRO · NET NOVEMBER — UNCONFIRMED

🇮🇳Gaganyaan G1: watch this space

Launch calendars list the uncrewed G1 test flight, carrying the humanoid robot Vyommitra, as no earlier than November — but ISRO hasn't confirmed a date and has said only that it is striving for end-2026, with safety checks setting the pace. A slip is possible.

CNSA · ONGOING

🇨🇳Tianwen-2 works at Kamoʻoalewa

China's asteroid mission continues at the quasi-moon, with a sample return to Earth planned for late 2027.

🛰️ November SkyWatch Quiz

Six questions from this month's episode.

Question 1 / 6

Activity

🔥 Fireball log

  • Watch 20 minutes in the late evening on Nov 4–5 or 11–12.
  • Note how many meteors you see — and how many were bright and slow.
  • Compare with the Leonids on Nov 18: which were faster?
Activity

📷 Mars–Jupiter dawn photo

  • Look east before sunrise on Nov 16 and find the bright pair near Regulus.
  • Photograph them with a phone on a steady surface.
  • Repeat on Nov 30 and see how far they've moved.
Activity

🌕 Supermoon size test

  • Photograph the full Moon on Nov 24 with the same zoom you used on a past full Moon.
  • Measure the Moon's width in each photo.
  • The supermoon should be a little larger — but is it enough to notice by eye?
